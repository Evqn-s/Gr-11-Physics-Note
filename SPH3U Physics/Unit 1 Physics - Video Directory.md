## Subject 1: Introductory Mathematical Skills

### Significant Digits and Rounding Rules in Physics
* **Resource:** [Significant Figures Made Easy!](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5U3Ks7_4-g4)
* **Topics Covered:** 
  - Rules for identifying significant digits (non-zero digits, leading zeros, captive zeros, trailing zeros with/without decimals).
  - Rounding standards in physical measurements.
  - Operational rules: Addition/Subtraction (fewest decimal places) vs. Multiplication/Division (fewest total significant figures).
* **Key Takeaway:** Always maintain full calculator precision during multi-step algebra and round only once at the final recorded answer.

### Rearranging Physics Formulas Step-by-Step
* **Resource:** [Rearranging Equations and Formulas - Step-by-Step Tutorial](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_W2n5r9QZcQ)
* **Topics Covered:** 
  - Applying inverse operations (addition/subtraction, multiplication/division, squaring/square roots).
  - Cross-multiplication techniques for fractions.
  - Isolating variables embedded inside brackets or square roots prior to numerical substitution.
* **Key Takeaway:** Isolate the target variable in pure algebraic symbols first before substituting numbers and units.

### Unit Conversion using Dimensional Analysis & MKS Units
* **Resource:** [Unit Conversion the Easy Way (Dimensional Analysis)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HRe1mire4Gc)
* **Topics Covered:** 
  - Fundamental SI/MKS base units: meter ($\text{m}$), kilogram ($\text{kg}$), second ($\text{s}$).
  - Setting up conversion factors as fractions equal to 1.
  - Metric prefixes ($\text{milli-}$, $\text{centi-}$, $\text{kilo-}$) and multi-step conversion chains (e.g., converting speed from $\text{km/h}$ to $\text{m/s}$ via $\div 3.6$).
* **Key Takeaway:** Structure unit conversions so that unwanted units cancel diagonally across fraction numerators and denominators.

## Subject 2: Scalar & 1D Vector Motion Foundations

### Physics Classroom: Distance versus Displacement
* **Resource:** [The Physics Classroom: Distance and Displacement](https://www.physicsclassroom.com/class/1DKin/Lesson-1/Distance-and-Displacement)
* **Topics Covered:** 
  - Differentiation between total ground covered (distance, a scalar) and overall change in position (displacement, a vector).
  - One-dimensional sign conventions and reference frames ($+$ for East/Right/North, $-$ for West/Left/South).
  - Line diagrams demonstrating round-trip journeys where total distance is positive while net displacement is zero ($\Delta\vec{d} = 0$).
* **Key Takeaway:** Displacement depends only on initial and final positions ($\Delta\vec{d} = \vec{d}_f - \vec{d}_i$), completely independent of the path taken.

### Physics Classroom: Speed and Velocity
* **Resource:** [Physics Classroom: Speed and Velocity Tutorial](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zhNzEj3Or98)
* **Topics Covered:** 
  - Defining speed as a scalar ($v = \text{distance} / \Delta t$).
  - Defining velocity as a vector quantity ($\vec{v} = \Delta\vec{d} / \Delta t$).
  - Understanding average speed vs. average velocity during directional turns and loops.
* **Key Takeaway:** Average speed is only equal to the magnitude of average velocity when motion occurs in an unbroken straight line without reversing direction.

### Metronome Walking Activity / Speedometer Analysis
* **Resources:** 
  - [Walking with a Metronome - Uniform Motion Demonstration](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AZNZgCX2y_M)
  - [Metronome Cadence & Kinematic Pacing](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dcbWC7DRS9o)
  - [Constant Speed vs Changing Speed Analysis](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=15v-U35V_nw)
* **Topics Covered:** 
  - Using fixed audible beats (cadence) to enforce constant velocity pacing.
  - Analyzing vehicle speedometers to illustrate instantaneous speed.
  - Proving that instantaneous speed equals average speed under uniform motion conditions.
* **Key Takeaway:** A speedometer measures instantaneous speed; it does not indicate motion direction or average journey velocity.

## Subject 3: Motion Graphs & Transition Graphs

### Graphing Position vs Time | Introduction to Kinematics
* **Resource:** [Graphing Position vs Time \| Introduction to Kinematics](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=e18KM681cmg)
* **Topics Covered:** 
  - Constructing position-time ($d-t$) graphs from experimental data tables.
  - Choosing appropriate scales, labelling coordinate axes with symbols and units, and plotting coordinate points.
  - Drawing linear lines of best fit to smooth out measurement uncertainty.
* **Key Takeaway:** The physical slope of a position-time graph directly represents the velocity of the moving object.

### Position-Time Graphs: Constant Speed Motion
* **Resource:** [Position-Time Graphs: Constant Speed Motion](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TxJH0ON610w)
* **Topics Covered:** 
  - Flat horizontal lines indicating zero velocity (stationary object).
  - Constant positive slopes representing uniform forward motion.
  - Constant negative slopes representing uniform backward motion.
  - Comparing steepness: steeper slopes indicate higher speed.
* **Key Takeaway:** A constant linear slope on a $d-t$ graph proves uniform motion with zero acceleration ($\vec{a} = 0$).

### Position-Time Graphs: Changing Speed Motion & Tangent Lines
* **Resource:** [Position-Time Graphs: Changing Speed Motion](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-U-0_D5NM4I)
* **Topics Covered:** 
  - Interpreting curve curvature (parabolas curving upwards vs. downwards).
  - Distinguishing between average velocity (secant line connecting two time coordinates) and instantaneous velocity (tangent line at a specific point).
  - Speeding up vs. slowing down visual curves.
* **Key Takeaway:** The tangent line slope at time $t$ equals the instantaneous velocity at that precise moment.

### How to Draw a Tangent Line
* **Resource:** [How to Draw a Tangent Line on a Curved Graph](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-Cii752uUkI)
* **Topics Covered:** 
  - Aligning a clear plastic ruler to touch the curve at exactly one point without slicing through the curve.
  - Balancing the visual angles on both sides of the contact point.
  - Extending the tangent line across the full graph grid to select two well-separated grid intersections for accurate slope calculation ($\text{slope} = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}$).
* **Key Takeaway:** Never use data points from the curve itself when finding the tangent slope; choose two distant coordinates located directly on the extended tangent line.

### Velocity-Time Graphs: Determining Slope & Acceleration
* **Resource:** [The Physics Classroom: Determining Slope on a v-t Graph](https://www.physicsclassroom.com/class/1DKin/Lesson-4/Determining-the-Slope-on-a-v-t-Graph)
* **Topics Covered:** 
  - Slope on a velocity-time ($v-t$) graph representing acceleration: $\vec{a} = \frac{\Delta\vec{v}}{\Delta t}$.
  - Geometric area between the velocity line and the time axis representing displacement ($\Delta\vec{d}$).
  - Positive areas above $v = 0$ representing forward displacement; negative areas below $v = 0$ representing backward displacement.
* **Key Takeaway:** When a velocity line crosses the time axis ($v = 0$), the object changes its direction of motion.

### Velocity to Position-Time Graph Tutorial & Simulation
* **Resources:** 
  - [Velocity to Position-Time Graph Tutorial](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vPhwP-w5tUU)
  - [oPhysics Kinematics Interactive Simulation](https://ophysics.com/k4b.html)
* **Topics Covered:** 
  - Reconstructing position coordinates from cumulative $v-t$ displacement areas.
  - Connecting discrete coordinates using straight lines for constant velocity intervals and parabolic arcs for constant acceleration intervals.
  - Constructing synchronized acceleration-time ($a-t$) graphs by plotting interval slopes.
* **Key Takeaway:** Area under $a-t$ gives change in velocity ($\Delta v$); area under $v-t$ gives displacement ($\Delta d$).

## Subject 4: Kinematic Equations & Advanced Kinematics

### EUREKA - Acceleration Part 2
* **Resource:** [EUREKA! Series - Acceleration (Episode 2)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uCG5jjzMJgU)
* **Topics Covered:** 
  - Intuitive visualization of rate of change of speed/velocity.
  - Units of acceleration ($\text{m/s}^2$ as "meters per second, every second").
  - Difference between maintaining high speed and experiencing acceleration.
* **Key Takeaway:** An object can have a velocity of zero and still experience non-zero acceleration (e.g., at the highest point of a vertical throw).

### Apollo 15 Moon Feather and Hammer Free-Fall Experiment (David Scott)
* **Resource:** [Apollo 15 Feather and Hammer Drop on the Moon - NASA / Science News](https://youtu.be/Oo8TaPVsn9Y)
* **Topics Covered:** 
  - Commander David Scott dropping a geological hammer ($1.32\text{ kg}$) and a falcon feather ($0.03\text{ kg}$) in the lunar vacuum.
  - Verification of Galileo's principle: all objects accelerate at the exact same rate in a vacuum regardless of mass.
  - Absence of atmospheric drag and terminal velocity.
* **Key Takeaway:** In the absence of air resistance, acceleration due to gravity is completely independent of mass, size, or shape.

### MIT / Science News Vacuum Chamber Free Fall Experiment
* **Resource:** [Brian Cox Visits the World's Biggest Vacuum Chamber - BBC / Science News](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=E43-CfukEgs)
* **Topics Covered:** 
  - NASA Space Power Facility drop test with bowling ball and feathers in standard air vs. ultra-high vacuum ($99.999\%$ air removed).
  - Ultra-high-speed camera capture showing identical trajectories in vacuum.
  - Direct physical demonstration of gravitational acceleration $\vec{g} = 9.81\text{ m/s}^2\text{ [down]}$.
* **Key Takeaway:** Heavy and light objects fall at identical rates when air resistance is eliminated.

### Chase & Collision Problem Solving Tutorial Video
* **Resources:** 
  - [Chase & Collision Problem Solving Walkthrough](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Fw83cXoWTkI)
  - [Spark Timer Tape Kinematics Analysis](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fOFH9JyGegc)
* **Topics Covered:** 
  - Step-by-step setup of two-body kinematics problems (pursuit and head-on collisions).
  - Constructing T-charts to segregate Object A and Object B variables.
  - Setting up position functions: $x_A(t) = x_B(t)$.
  - Accounting for head starts (distance offsets) and delayed start times ($t_B = t_A - \Delta t$).
  - Solving the resulting quadratic equation using $t = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}$.
* **Key Takeaway:** At the exact instant of a chase catch-up or collision, both bodies occupy the exact same spatial position at the exact same clock time.
