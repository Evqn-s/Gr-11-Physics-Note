### Subject 3: Motion Graphs & Transition Graphs ($d-t$, $v-t$, $a-t$)
* **Deep-Dive Guide:** [[Subject 3 - Graphical Analysis Guide|Subject 3: Motion Graphs & Transition Graphs (d-t, v-t, a-t)]]
* **Quick Summary Sheet:** [[Subject 3 - Motion Graphs Summary]]
* **Core Topics Covered:**
  - Position-time ($d-t$) graphs: linear slope = constant velocity, secant slope = average velocity, tangent slope = instantaneous velocity
  - Velocity-time ($v-t$) graphs: slope = acceleration, signed geometric area under curve = displacement ($\Delta\vec{d}$)
  - Directional states on $v-t$ graphs: forward vs. backward, speeding up vs. slowing down, zero crossings (instant of reversal)
  - Transition graph mechanics:
    - Slopes: $d-t \xrightarrow{\text{slope}} v-t \xrightarrow{\text{slope}} a-t$
    - Areas: $a-t \xrightarrow{\text{area}} v-t \xrightarrow{\text{area}} d-t$
  - Connecting discrete position points with straight linear lines (uniform motion) or smooth parabolas (accelerated motion)

## 4. Unit 1 Test Mastery Checklist

Use this comprehensive checklist to audit your conceptual understanding, problem-solving skills, and mathematical rigor prior to taking the **Unit 1 Test (Part 1: 1D Kinematics)**.

### Algebraic & Mathematical Foundation Skills
- [ ] Count significant digits accurately across standard numbers, trailing zeros with/without decimals, and scientific notation.
- [ ] Apply the decimal place rule for addition/subtraction and the least total SD rule for multiplication/division.
- [ ] Round only once at the final step of a multi-stage calculation; carry unrounded intermediate values in calculator memory.
- [ ] Rearrange multi-variable physics equations algebraically before substituting numerical quantities.
- [ ] Execute multi-step dimensional analysis unit conversions using unit cancellation fractions (e.g., $\text{km/h} \to \text{m/s}$, $\text{min} \to \text{s}$, $\text{cm} \to \text{m}$).
- [ ] Calculate percentage error for experimental accuracy against accepted values:
  $$\% \text{Error} = \left\vert\frac{\text{Exp} - \text{Acc}}{\text{Acc}}\right\vert \times 100\%$$
- [ ] Calculate percentage difference for experimental precision between repeated measurements:
  $$\% \text{Diff} = \frac{\vert\text{Meas}_1 - \text{Meas}_2\vert}{\text{Average}} \times 100\%$$

### Vector & Motion Foundation Skills
- [ ] Clearly categorize physical quantities as scalars (mass, time, distance, speed) or vectors (position, displacement, velocity, acceleration).
- [ ] Write vector quantities using correct physical notation: magnitude, physical unit, and spatial direction in square brackets (e.g., $\vec{v} = 24.5\text{ m/s [E]}$).
- [ ] Establish explicit 1D sign conventions at the start of every problem (e.g., "Let $[\text{North}] = +$", "Let $[\text{Up}] = +$").
- [ ] Differentiate between total distance traveled ($d$, path length) and net displacement ($\Delta\vec{d}$, straight-line change in position).
- [ ] Calculate average speed ($v_{\text{avg}} = d/\Delta t$) and average velocity ($\vec{v}_{\text{avg}} = \Delta\vec{d}/\Delta t$) for multi-interval journeys.
- [ ] Construct accurate, well-scaled 1D line diagrams showing the origin ($0$), initial position vectors, and sequential displacement vectors.

### Graphical Analysis & Transition Graph Skills
- [ ] Determine instantaneous velocity from a linear position-time ($d-t$) graph using rise-over-run slope calculations.
- [ ] Construct tangent lines on curved $d-t$ graphs: align the straightedge cleanly at the single point of contact, balance visual angles, extend the line across grid intersections, and calculate the slope to obtain instantaneous velocity ($\vec{v}_{\text{inst}}$).
- [ ] Calculate average velocity on a curved $d-t$ graph by drawing a secant line between initial and final time coordinates.
- [ ] Determine acceleration from a velocity-time ($v-t$) graph by calculating the slope of the line: $\vec{a} = \frac{\Delta\vec{v}}{\Delta t}$.
- [ ] Determine displacement from a $v-t$ graph by calculating the geometric area under the curve, partitioning into rectangles, triangles, and trapezoids.
- [ ] Correctly apply positive displacement for areas above the time axis ($v > 0$) and negative displacement for areas below the time axis ($v < 0$).
- [ ] Identify motion states on $v-t$ graphs: moving forward vs. backward, speeding up (moving away from $v=0$) vs. slowing down (moving toward $v=0$), and turnaround points ($v=0$ crossing).
- [ ] Construct complete transition graphs ($d-t \leftrightarrow v-t \leftrightarrow a-t$):
  - Differentiate slopes from $d-t$ to form $v-t$, and slopes from $v-t$ to form $a-t$.
  - Calculate cumulative areas from $a-t$ to determine $\Delta v$, and from $v-t$ to determine $\Delta d$.
  - Connect points with straight lines for uniform motion and parabolic curves for uniformly accelerated motion.

### Kinematic Equations & Advanced Problem-Solving Skills
- [ ] Apply the "3-4-5 Rule": identify the 5 kinematic variables ($\vec{v}_i, \vec{v}_f, \vec{a}, \Delta\vec{d}, \Delta t$), extract 3 knowns from the problem statement, and select the correct Big 5 equation to isolate the target unknown.
- [ ] Solve free-fall problems under gravity ($\vec{g} = 9.81\text{ m/s}^2\text{ [down]}$):
  - Dropped from rest: $\vec{v}_i = 0$.
  - Thrown upwards: $\vec{v}_{\text{top}} = 0$ at maximum height; symmetry of upward and downward travel times and speeds.
  - Thrown downwards: $\vec{v}_i < 0$ (with $[\text{Up}] = +$).
- [ ] Solve consecutive interval (multi-stage) problems:
  - Divide problem into distinct motion intervals.
  - Seamlessly pass final velocity of stage 1 as initial velocity of stage 2 ($\vec{v}_{f1} = \vec{v}_{i2}$).
  - Sum individual stage displacements to obtain total displacement ($\Delta\vec{d}_{\text{total}} = \Delta\vec{d}_1 + \Delta\vec{d}_2$).
- [ ] Solve chase and collision two-body problems:
  - Construct organized T-charts for Body $A$ and Body $B$.
  - Formulate algebraic position functions $x_A(t)$ and $x_B(t)$ with appropriate time delays ($\Delta t_B = t - t_{\text{delay}}$).
  - Set position functions equal ($x_A(t) = x_B(t)$) and solve for time using the quadratic formula:
    $$t = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}$$
  - Substitute the valid positive time back into either position equation to determine the exact location of catch-up or impact.
